# 10 — What to pursue, and what could kill it

## Research recommendation

Pursue **a securities-history and claim-evidence system**, with Track B's family/inheritance journey as the user-facing entry point. Retain acquisition reconstruction as the technically substantive engine and post-transmission continuity as a distinctive outcome.

This is a recommendation about the strongest research direction given the evidence, not a guarantee of hackathon selection or market success.

## Ranked wedges within the direction

| Rank | Wedge | Why it is promising | Main weakness | Concrete proof required |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Uncontested inherited-security evidence pack with reconstructed history | Human story, current rights, document complexity, measurable output | Could degrade into a form checklist | Expert-reviewed case resolved from messy inputs, with traceable quantity/history and correct route |
| 2 | Broken acquisition history after transfer | Best documented broker gap; easiest deterministic ₹ demonstration | Strong MProfit overlap; may feel tax/accounting-focused | Incomplete-history case that existing tools require substantial manual work to solve |
| 3 | Old physical certificate → current evidence chain | Immediate visual story and technically rich entity/event history | Title verification and RTA cooperation remain external | One issuer lineage checked against institutional records |
| 4 | Deficiency-response reconciliation | Directly useful when submitted documents are repeatedly questioned | Risks becoming a generic grievance assistant | Actual rejection/deficiency letter matched to submitted facts and applicable requirements |
| 5 | Nominee-to-heir movement classification audit | Regulator-confirmed historical problem; links inheritance and reporting | TLH reform already addresses it; current failure rate unknown | Evidence of a remaining post-implementation mismatch with a correct baseline |

The rankings are qualitative judgments, not an invented numeric scoring model.

## Jury objections and honest answers

| Objection | Evidence-led response |
|---|---|
| “MProfit already does this.” | It covers much of the ledger. Demonstrate the specific incomplete-evidence and claim-document task, or concede the novelty is weak. |
| “SEBI has already simplified transmission.” | Correct. Make current rights usable on a difficult document pack; do not pitch repealed friction. |
| “This is just OCR plus a chatbot.” | Show deterministic event reconstruction, constraints, conflicting evidence and refusal on ambiguity. |
| “Where is the money saved?” | Show reviewed computation differences or measured reduced rework. Actual assets credited require external confirmation. |
| “Where does the data come from?” | User-selected records and authoritative public documents for the bounded case. No imaginary all-market API. |
| “Who decides the heir?” | The system records supported roles and routes; it does not adjudicate contested succession. |
| “What if the original price is gone?” | It remains unresolved unless a valid legal/evidentiary method applies. No fabricated reconstruction. |
| “Why not use the new IEPF portal?” | Use it for its purpose. Test whether upstream history and documentary reconciliation remain burdensome. |
| “Can a smaller-town family operate it?” | This remains untested until a target user completes the task; English PDFs and technical dashboards are not sufficient. |

## The smallest useful field study

Seek permission for anonymised evidence from 6–10 cases, including easy successes and failures. This is a proposed next study; no outreach has been sent.

- At least two ordinary broker transfers, including one with complete old records.
- Two uncontested transmissions with different documentation routes.
- One physical-share lineage case.
- One case with a deficiency letter or conflicting records.
- If possible, one case existing software resolves well and one genuinely impossible case.

Have a securities-operations professional review quantity/movement facts and a qualified tax/legal professional review the relevant interpretations. Record disagreement explicitly.

## Interview questions that elicit evidence

1. What were you trying to do, and what exact document or field stopped you?
2. Which records did you have at the start? Which were later obtained?
3. What did the institution ask for, in writing, and on what date?
4. Did the problem concern ownership, quantity, acquisition cost, identity, or procedure?
5. What did you try with the old broker, DP, RTA, CAS and existing software?
6. How many active work sessions, visits and resubmissions were required?
7. What changed after the latest regulatory simplification?
8. Was money actually overpaid or an asset actually credited? What verifies it?
9. Which steps could a family member complete without professional judgment?
10. Would a portable evidence pack have reduced work, or was missing institutional data the real barrier?

## Stop/go gates

**Go:** real document packs exist; supported facts can be reconstructed; an existing-tool baseline shows material manual work; the current route is independently reviewed; target users understand the result.

**Narrow:** only corporate-action mathematics is demonstrably better; drop unsupported inheritance claims and present the narrower contribution.

**Stop/pivot within this direction:** competitors solve the same task readily, cases mostly require legal adjudication, or no evidence can distinguish possible histories.

## Larger-system possibilities

The same verified event/evidence layer could serve accountant hand-offs, DP/RTA exception processing, investor-controlled historical records and professional claim preparation. These are hypotheses for later validation. Commercial distribution, liability, source licensing and institutional acceptance are unresolved.
